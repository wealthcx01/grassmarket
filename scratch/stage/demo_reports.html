Two brokerages — Revolut and Hargreaves Lansdown — assessed end-to-end through the studio, from pipeline to a finalised, scored report. This is what the advisor and the client would see.

Verified engine-computed, not fabricated. Every score here was reproduced in a clean-room recompute of the locked inputs using the ATLAS engine — Revolut recomputed to 60.49, HL to 57.17, matching the studio's portfolio scores exactly. What the build agent supplied was the input ratings (read from the public brokerage reviews, as an advisor would enter them); every score, report, and commission below was produced by the deployed system.

Read this first — what this is and isn't

  • Simulated, not a real engagement. Inputs were authored from public app reviews of these firms — not a Bruntsfield assessment of the actual company.
  • Sandbox & watermarked. Both finalised in sandbox mode: self-approved, not client-facing, every page stamped “DRAFT — not client-usable.”
  • Coefficients are draft. The weights are v1-draft-pending-elicitation (placeholder, review due Dec 2026). Treat the numbers as directional, not final.
  • One known inconsistency. The portfolio list shows the deterministic V (60.5 / 57.2); the report headline shows the Monte-Carlo median V (58.8 / 56.5). Two legitimate numbers, two surfaces — being reconciled (ticketed).
  • Customer-Proposition (C) not scored. Under retail draft coefficients C is unweighted, so the 39 C-inputs entered were stored but returned no C index.
